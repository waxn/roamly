# Cell coverage

_Read when: CellSample, towers, carriers, CellScanner, Cell signal page._

**Cell coverage (which towers you connect to, per SIM).** Roamly recorded where you were but nothing about how you were connected. The phone logs its serving and neighbour cells alongside each GPS fix — signal strength, band, RAT and which SIM — and `/map/` gains two optional layers. **Off by default on the phone and off by default on the map** (migration `0089`).

Structurally this is **Health Connect, not a `Location` extension**: an optional, separately-permissioned, off-by-default stream with its own table, endpoint, cache generation, upload path and backup section. Extra keys on `LocationPushPayload` were rejected for three reasons beyond precedent — the cardinality doesn't fit (1-to-many per fix, on a different clock, so most fixes carry zero cell rows and some carry four); `push_location_batch`'s `bulk_create(ignore_conflicts=True)` **skips a re-pushed fix wholesale, and would take its cell payload with it**, which the uploader's 4xx fallback and `replace=true` recovery hit routinely; and `push_location`/`push_location_batch` are the two endpoints deliberately excluded from `_require_api_intent` because GPSLogger and OwnTracks post to them, so widening them entangles our schema with theirs.

**ONE TABLE, AND NO DERIVED-TOWER TABLE.** `cell_towers_api` is a `GROUP BY` over `CellSample` — signal-weighted centroid, first/last seen, sample count, best/worst dBm — so a tower can never drift from the samples that produced it and there is no rebuild job. A second table would be a cache with no invalidation story. Three details in that aggregation are load-bearing: **`.order_by()` with no arguments is mandatory** (the absent `Meta.ordering` is what makes it correct, and the explicit empty call protects against a future pre-ordered queryset appending columns to the `GROUP BY` and shattering it into one group per row); **`dbm__isnull=False`**, since one NULL propagates through `Sum` and NULLs the whole centroid; and **`Greatest(dbm + 140, 1.0)`**, because a pathological −145 dBm reading would otherwise produce a zero or negative weight and *invert* the centroid, pulling the tower away from where the signal was strongest. `tracker_cs_user_cell_idx` carries the grouping key in the same column order, which is what makes it a grouped index scan; it still reads every row for that user, hence the 1h cache.

**The phone-side gate bounds the cost, and it is tuned — the first version was tuned wrong.** Serving plus neighbours on a dual-SIM phone is ~8-12 `CellInfo` entries per reading; taken once per fix at the 30s interval that is **~29,000 rows/day, ~10M/year — ten times `Location` itself**, for a feature that is off by default. `CellScanner` bounds it two ways: `CELL_MIN_SCAN_INTERVAL_MS` (60s) decouples scanning from the capture cadence, so dropping to a 2s interval to record a ride does *not* multiply cell rows; and a per-cell gate writes a row only when that cell is newly seen, **or** you have moved `CELL_MIN_MOVE_M` (150m) since its last sample, **or** its heartbeat elapsed (`CELL_HEARTBEAT_MS` 3 min serving / `CELL_NEIGHBOUR_HEARTBEAT_MS` 10 min neighbour, capped at `CELL_MAX_NEIGHBOURS` 8 per SIM per scan). That is ~2,100 rows/day sitting still and ~3,000/day on a mixed day, against the 2,880 `Location` already writes at a 30s interval.

**Two clauses were removed after the first real run produced four readings in twenty minutes**, and both failures are worth keeping written down. The heartbeats were 10 and 30 minutes, so a stationary phone recorded its serving cell twice an hour per SIM. And neighbours **only rode along in a scan where their SIM's serving cell also qualified** — which reads like a volume bound and is really a mute button, since the serving cell is exactly what a stationary phone holds back. Neighbours are now gated on their own heartbeat and nothing else. A neighbour with **no global cell identity is also no longer dropped**: it cannot become a *tower*, but `cell_towers_api` already filters on `cid`, so discarding it on the phone threw away a real signal reading at a real place — which is most of what the signal and coverage layers are for — in exchange for nothing. It is counted as `CELL_NO_IDENTITY` rather than discarded. There is still deliberately **no** "signal changed ≥N dB" clause: indoor RSRP wanders ±10 dB on a phone sitting still, so even at a 6 dB threshold it fires most scans for every visible cell, which is a multiple of the whole budget above.

**The scan never runs on the capture path.** `savePoint` offers the position to a **`Channel.CONFLATED`** (`cellScans`) drained by its own consumer, mirroring `startPointWriter()`. A cell scan is several binder calls plus, on API 29+, an async callback with a timeout — strictly more expensive than the battery read that already sits outside `captureLock` for exactly this reason; inline it would reproduce the failure the point writer exists to prevent (slow work widening the window in which arriving fixes are judged against stale filter state and **rejected outright**). CONFLATED is deliberately the opposite choice from `pointWrites`: every captured fix must land, but a backed-up scan queue only wants the *freshest* position, since scanning against a stale one attributes a tower to where the phone no longer is.

**`READ_PHONE_STATE` is optional and is only the multi-SIM upgrade.** `getAllCellInfo()` needs only `ACCESS_FINE_LOCATION`, which tracking already holds; `SubscriptionManager.getActiveSubscriptionInfoList()` is what needs the permission. Requested separately at runtime with its own rationale (the Health Connect pattern), and declining leaves the toggle **on**, falling back to the default subscription with `sim_slot = -1`. Subscription id is **not** stored (unstable across SIM re-insertion and eSIM reprovisioning, so it would fragment a tower's history); **IMSI/ICCID/IMEI are never read** — PII the app has no business keeping, and on API 29+ they need `READ_PRIVILEGED_PHONE_STATE`, which a sideloaded app cannot hold.

**All telephony lives in `tracking/CellScanner.kt` and nowhere else**, the containment rule `LocationSource.kt` applies to Play Services types. Its hazards: the **`CellInfo.UNAVAILABLE` sentinel** appears in nearly every `CellIdentity*` getter and a missed one writes `2147483647` into a column where it reads as a real TAC (hence `orNull()` on *every* getter, no exceptions); **`getBands()` is API 30+ and frequently returns an EMPTY array even there**, because plenty of modems never report it — which made "no band" the common case rather than the old-phone case. For **LTE** the fallback is exact rather than a guess: 3GPP 36.101 gives each band a fixed, non-overlapping block of downlink EARFCNs, so `LTE_BANDS` determines the band from the channel number with no ambiguity (new bands are appended; existing ranges do not move). There is deliberately **no NR equivalent** — 38.104 lets several n-bands share NRARFCN ranges (n77/n78, n2/n25), so a channel number genuinely does not determine the band and a table would be inventing an answer; NR band stays null unless `getBands()` supplies one; `getAllCellInfo()` is **throttled on Android 10+ and answers from cache**, so API 29+ uses `requestCellInfoUpdate` and both paths drop readings older than `CELL_MAX_AGE_MS`; **`CellInfoCdma` is skipped entirely**; and **neighbours with no global cell identity are dropped** (they cannot become a tower and cannot be told apart from a PCI reused two towns over), counted as `CELL_NO_IDENTITY` so the drop is visible.

**Room `TrackingDatabase` v3 → v4** holds `cell_samples` — an un-uploaded sample exists nowhere else, so it belongs in the one database with **no destructive-migration fallback**. `MIGRATION_3_4` is purely additive and is the first migration written since that guarantee was documented, so **the regression test that matters is: capture points offline on the current APK, upgrade in place, confirm the backlog still uploads.** The DAO deliberately exposes **no `Flow`** — `SettingsViewModel`'s collectors run against this database in the same process as the capture loop, which is why it is excluded from Activity-scoping. Upload is a **second drain loop inside the existing `UploadWorker`**, not a second periodic job, so cell rows ride the same radio wake-up; a 404/405 marks the batch synced **without uploading** (an older server will never accept them, and the alternative is a table that grows forever), counted as `CELL_UPLOAD_UNSUPPORTED`.

**A 2xx is not proof the rows landed, and treating it as proof deleted data.** `cell_samples_push` skips rows it cannot parse with a `continue` and still answers 200, and its `bulk_create` fallback used to swallow every per-row failure in a bare `except: pass` and answer 200 as well — so one systematic problem (a NOT NULL column, a bad coercion) made the phone mark the batch synced and drop it, which is how a phone holding thousands faced a server holding hundreds. Three things close that: the server **logs why rows were skipped** and returns `skipped`, it returns **500 when nothing at all stored** so the phone retries rather than discards, and the uploader **reads `accepted`** and bumps `CELL_UPLOAD_REJECTED` by the shortfall. Rejected rows are still marked synced — re-sending malformed bytes forever would wedge the queue behind rows that can never land — so the counter *is* the safeguard, and `CaptureStats.lastCellUploadError` records the reason (shown in Diagnostics) because a counter cannot distinguish "server down" from "server too old" from "auth wrong". **The cell drain also runs after the points drain bails out**: both of that loop's early returns used to sit above it, so one transient point error skipped every cell batch and the backlog simply grew. Auth failure is the one exception, since nothing uploads without it.

**A CELL IS NOT A TOWER.** `cell_towers_api` groups by **site** (`cell_utils.site_key`), not by cell: one mast carries ~3 sectors on several bands each, so one row per cell turned four visible masts into twenty-six markers *and* split each site's readings across a dozen separate, weaker position estimates. For **LTE** the split is exact rather than heuristic — the 28-bit E-UTRAN Cell Identifier is an eNodeB ID in the top 20 bits and a Cell ID in the bottom 8 (36.413), and an eNodeB ID is unique within a PLMN, so every sector and band of a site shares `cid >> 8`. There is deliberately **no NR equivalent**: the 36-bit NCI splits at a boundary that is *configurable* (22-32 bits) and broadcast in SIB1, which we do not have, so any shift would silently merge unrelated sites or fail to merge one. GSM and WCDMA identifiers carry no dependable site structure either, so both stay ungrouped. The dense sample payload interns a `site` id alongside the cell id purely so the tower popup's "show this tower's readings" can filter on the mast rather than on one of its twelve cells.

**THE CENTROID OF YOUR OBSERVATIONS IS NOT THE TOWER**, and drawing it as though it were is what put towers inside people's houses. If every reading of a cell came from one address, the signal-weighted centroid *is* that address; no estimator can do better, because the readings constrain the tower's **distance** (weakly, via signal strength) and say nothing whatever about its **direction**. `cell_utils.estimate_tower` therefore returns a `confidence`: below `MIN_SPREAD_M` (250m) of observation spread it is **`unconstrained`**, carries a `range_m` from the path-loss table, and the map draws a **ring** at that radius with a hollow centre — the centre marks where *you* were. **Spread alone is not constraint**, which is the second thing that made placed towers poor: driving past a mast on one road gives a kilometre of spread and two opposite bearings, and the centroid lands *on the road* while the mast sits off to one side — a confident-looking dot wrong in the one direction the data never sampled. `bearing_arcs` counts how many of 8 compass sectors the readings fall into as seen from the centroid, and `MIN_ARCS_FOR_GOOD` (3) gates the `good` verdict. Above `GOOD_SPREAD_M` (1200m) **with enough arcs**, `_refine` runs a **bounded** gradient descent against the per-reading distance estimates, clamped to the observation spread because the path-loss model is rough enough that an unconstrained solve runs away. Spread but too few arcs is **`rough`** and is deliberately *not* refined — sharpening a number that is wrong sideways just makes it confidently wrong. `cell_utils.distance_from_dbm` is calibrated for LTE RSRP on an urban macro cell and is wrong by a factor of two in either direction — it exists **only** to say "roughly a kilometre away, direction unknown", never to be presented as a measured distance. The spread itself comes from a running bounding box, so the scan keeps at most `_CELL_TOWER_MAX_POINTS` (400) strided readings per cell rather than all of them.

**Map: three toggles, not one** (`roamly_cell_signal`, `roamly_cell_coverage`, `roamly_cell_towers`, all default off), because they obey different clocks — signal and coverage follow the range picker, the tower layer is **always all-time** (a tower you once saw stays one, the argument fog of war makes for itself). Plus **`roamly_hide_track`**, which hides the GPS layers so the cell layers can be read alone; it reuses replay's existing `setBaseLayersVisible`, and **both callers go through `applyBaseLayerVisibility()`** — otherwise ending a replay would silently un-hide a track the user deliberately hid, and a basemap swap would too (hence its `styledata` registration).

**Colour is carrier (hue) × signal (opacity).** The hues are assigned **server-side** in `cell_utils.build_carrier_legend` and ride in every payload's `carriers` legend, so the map layers and the Cell page cannot disagree about which network is which. `carrier_key` matches on the **modem-reported name first** (what the user recognises, and what an MVNO reports when riding a host network) and falls back to a deliberately small US MCC/MNC table — a complete registry is thousands of rows that go stale, and a carrier missing from it still renders, just in a generic colour. Verizon/T-Mobile/AT&T get brand hues; anything else draws from a generic palette ordered by sample count. Opacity carries signal on the 3GPP RSRP thresholds — the same functional-encoding reasoning as the speed gradient, and the reason the `--clay-*` categorical tokens are **not** used for it.

The dense layer is a **GL circle layer, not a canvas**: fog of war uses a canvas because a world polygon with tens of thousands of *holes* cannot be tessellated, which is a tessellation problem rather than a feature-count one. The **coverage** layer is a grid of ~139m squares (`_COVERAGE_CELLS_PER_DEG` 800) at **one row per (square, carrier)** — deliberately not a hull or an interpolated surface, which would claim coverage across ground that was never measured, and deliberately not collapsed to a dominant carrier, since a dual-SIM user's whole question is how the two networks differ. The tower ring's pixel radius uses the **per-feature zoom-stop trick** the dwell blobs document: MapLibre requires `['zoom']` to be the input of a *top-level* interpolate, so a pixel floor cannot be applied by wrapping it in `['max']` — that throws and silently kills the layer, so the floor is baked into `px_z0…px_z20` instead. Every layer is registered on **`style.load` *and* `styledata`** — skipping the second is the known trap and presents as "works until you switch basemap, then never again". Clicking a sample, a coverage square or a tower opens a popup; the tower popup's "show this tower's readings" **filters the already-loaded dense source** (`map.setFilter`) rather than adding an endpoint.

**Data → Cell signal** (`/cells/`, `cells_view` → `cells.html`) is one page off **one** endpoint, `cell_insights_api`: network shares with average/best/worst dBm, an RSRP quality histogram, RAT and band breakdowns, and the weakest measured squares. One endpoint rather than five because every figure comes from the same scan, and splitting them would walk the history five times for one page load. **Weak spots are scored on the *best* reading in each square, not the average** — somewhere you once got a good signal is not a dead zone, however many bad readings you also took indoors — and squares under three readings are dropped. No map on the page (`map_css`/`map_js` blocked out); rows deep-link to `/map/?lat=&lng=&zoom=`, which `map-core.js` already handles.

Cell reads sit behind their own **`cell_gen:{user}`** counter (`_bust_cell_cache`), never `cache_gen` — same reasoning as health and activities in both directions. Cell history is user content the phone cannot re-derive (the modem keeps no log), so it joins the backup format, `meta.version` **15 → 16**, streamed row-by-row in both directions. **New model ⇒ build + migrate; mobile change ⇒ rebuild APK + tag.**
