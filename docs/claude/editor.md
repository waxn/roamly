# Map editor, trash, gap fill

_Read when: /editor/, relabel, routing, trash, single missed-fix fill._

**Map editor** (`/editor/`, `editor_view` → `editor.html`, migration `0052`). Replaces automatic gap filling, which was removed: a background job deciding on its own which holes to fill, and how, produced output that could not be trusted, and four rounds of tuning did not fix the premise. Everything is now deliberate, synchronous and undoable — no job row, no thread, no nightly sweep.

The page is `standalone: True` (no nav chrome) and gets its rendering from **`tracker/static/tracker/map-core.js`**, shared with `/map/`, so the two look identical by construction rather than imitation. Tools: **select** (click; Ctrl-click toggles; Shift-drag box-selects via `queryRenderedFeatures` with a bbox; the **pencil** ("draw to select") paints a stroke with pointer events — mouse, pen or touch — and selects every rendered point within the brush radius of the path: the same bbox hit-test, then a per-point distance-to-polyline check in screen pixels; Ctrl/⌘ adds, Alt removes, Esc exits, brush size remembered in `roamly_editor_brush`. Like the box, it can only catch points that are **rendered**, so zoom in past the detail zoom to get every point; Delete/Backspace triggers "delete selected", which still confirms), **delete**, **connect via roads** (a start and an end point — the earliest and latest of ≥2 selected — routed *through* every recorded fix between them, see below), **move/retime** one point, **add** a point at a typed time or a **run** from an anchor at a fixed interval, and **copy a stretch** onto another date.

**Filtering the editor to a place.** The sidebar's **filter by place** search (`GET /api/editor/places/?q=`) offers towns, states and countries from the labels on the user's own points (a whole-history `GROUP BY`, cached 10 min under the plain key `editplaces:{user}` because `cache_gen` moves on every push) plus `Boundary` rows: counties, regions abroad and **town limits**, which match by polygon rather than by label and so find points inside a town whatever they are labelled. Picking one sets map-core's `placeFilter` (so every map request narrows exactly as on `/map/`, and `?city=&state=&country=&boundary=` deep-links work here too), sets the date range to the first and last day there, and lists **the dates you were there** (`GET /api/editor/places/days/`): local days merged into runs of consecutive days, since a home town would otherwise be a thousand rows, each one a click to narrow to. Editor-generated points are hidden while a place is set, because they carry no labels for it to match.

**Bulk relabelling** (migration `0110`). **change town / state** rewrites `city`/`state`/`country` (plus the derived `country_code` and `place_name`) on the selection, or on **every point under the current filter** (`POST /api/editor/relabel/`, with `/preview/` for the count and current labels). The filter scope reads `buildFilterParams()` server-side, so it reaches the points the map thinned out of view, and it **requires a place** so a bulk edit can never cover a whole history by accident. A blank field keeps each point's own value. A typed country must be a real one (`offline_geocode.country_by_name`), because Scratch and Achievements join on its code. Coordinates never change. `_after_relabel` busts `cache_gen` and `editplaces:` and recomputes the stats snapshot; `Visit` rows keep their old copies of the labels, exactly as after a re-geocode, and a place-filtered All-time overview on `/map/` can stay stale for its 1h cache.

**Each relabel is one `EditBatch` (`kind='relabel'`), undone from recent edits.** `EditBatch.label_undo` stores the labels before and after with the ids, per set of points that shared them; undo only touches points still showing what that batch set, so a newer relabel survives it. It can reach megabytes on a home town, so `editor_batches_api` defers it, and it is **excluded from backups** like every other `target='location'` batch.

**Hand-set labels survive a re-geocode, `rebuild` included.** `regeocode()` skips the ids in live relabel batches (`editor_tasks.relabelled_ids`), since otherwise the next boundary-backed run would quietly put the wrong town back. There is deliberately no column on `Location` for this; undoing the batch releases the points. **"Overwrite labels I set by hand"** on the Settings card (`overwrite_hand`, `--overwrite-hand-labels`) is the way past it, independent of `rebuild`: those points take the geocoder's answer unconditionally, and their relabel batches are marked undone in the same transaction, because every such point is now geocoded and a live batch would keep protecting a label nobody set by hand. Stopping the run rolls that back too. A backup restore mints new ids, so the labels survive it but this protection does not.

**Connecting routes through what was recorded.** A spotty drive still logged a fix now and then, and routing the two ends straight to each other ignored them — the road chosen could miss them, and the fill was timed as one steady pace. `_route_through_rows` takes every fix on that device strictly between the two ends (excluding `flag='suspect'` and accuracy > 100m, the distance-accounting bar) as a waypoint, and `editor_tasks.plan_through` routes leg by leg, each leg timed by its own two timestamps and Doppler speeds (`points_through` → `points_along` per leg). Real fixes are kept untouched; only the gaps get generated points. Legs shorter than the **"fill gaps longer than"** slider in the route panel (`min_gap_s`, default `LEG_MIN_S` 60s, clamped server-side to `LEG_MIN_S_BOUNDS` 5–900s, stepped values, remembered in `roamly_editor_min_gap_s`) or `_LEG_MIN_M` are ordinary tracking and are neither routed nor filled — otherwise a well-tracked stretch would cost a route call per fix. Moving the slider re-runs the preview (stale responses dropped by a request counter); a failed re-preview clears the candidates so an old plan can't be committed under a setting it wasn't built with; `MAX_ROUTED_LEGS` (150) caps the rest. A waypoint the router can't reach (usually a glitch off the network) is **skipped** rather than failing the connection, and reported; an unroutable leg under `_LEG_STRAIGHT_OK_M` (250m) is drawn as a chord. With no fixes in between, the old 3-alternative choice is unchanged; with waypoints there is one candidate, since the fixes pin the route down. **Repeated fixes are stepped over.** A phone without a fix mid-drive re-logs its last position with fresh timestamps (dwell points), so the data reads "parked at A for five minutes, then 3km to B in fifteen seconds" — and that leg had no room for a single point, which is why fills first came out empty. When a leg implies more than `_PLAUSIBLE_MPS` (50 m/s) and starts at the tail of a run of fixes within `_SAME_SPOT_M` (30m) of each other (`_same_spot_runs`), it is re-anchored to the run's *first* fix; the repeats are reported (`held`) but left in place. A real stop followed by a normal drive has a plausible departure leg and is untouched. Separately, `points_through` tightens a leg's spacing to `dt/2` when it is shorter than two `FILL_INTERVAL_S`, so any leg chosen for filling gets at least one point. The cache holds the leg plan plus every fix id, and commit re-reads all of them — a point deleted or moved since the preview makes it stale.

**Connecting is a preview, not a commit.** For many gaps there is more than one plausible way round and only the person who drove it knows which, so `/api/editor/route/` returns up to three candidates (drawn in distinct colours, clickable on the map or in the sidebar) and `/api/editor/route/commit/` lays down the one chosen by index. Candidates are held in the cache for 15 minutes and committed by index rather than by geometry, so a client can never inject a path that was never offered. Local alternatives come from the **penalty method** (`_local_alternatives`): A\* returns the single cheapest path, so asking again yields the same answer — inflating the cost of the edges each route used by `_ALT_PENALTY` (4×) pushes the next search onto a genuinely different road, and near-duplicates are rejected at `_ALT_SIMILARITY` (0.75 shared vertices). OSRM and Mapbox just ask for `alternatives=true`.

**Editor routing uses a much wider anchor tolerance** than `_ROUTE_MAX_ANCHOR_M` (80m): `_EDITOR_ANCHOR_M` is 500m. That gate exists to stop a *background job* inventing a leg from a point nowhere near a road, but here a person picked both ends deliberately. With the tight gate, connecting only worked for points close enough to a road to have been snappable in the first place — which is why it appeared to depend on snapping. `_nearest_edge` takes a matching `reach` so the ~1.1km index cells are searched widely enough to find the road.

**Point spacing follows a speed gradient.** One point per `FILL_INTERVAL_S` (10s) of travel, positioned by assuming speed varies linearly between the two anchors' recorded Doppler values: `v(t) = v0 + (v1-v0)·t/T`, so `s(t) = v0·t + (v1-v0)·t²/(2T)` scaled to the route length. Points therefore bunch where the track was slow and stretch where it was fast, instead of sitting at even distances. With no usable speed at either end it degrades to even spacing. The old 60s spacing with a flat 500-point cap put 500 points on a two-minute hop; 10s spacing means a short connection gets a handful.

A time interval alone cannot bound density, though, so `MIN_POINT_SPACING_M` (25m) is a hard floor applied *while* laying points down: a long gap over a short distance means 10 seconds of travel is only a few metres, and 40 minutes over 2km produced 239 points 8m apart — a solid band, denser than any real tracking. Thinning happens as points are placed rather than afterwards, because under a speed ramp the spacing varies along the route and only a running check catches the slow end; dropping one never moves the others, since each kept point stays where its own timestamp puts it. That case now yields 80 points at 25m. `MAX_POINTS_PER_ROUTE` (500) remains as the outer ceiling.

**Editor-generated points render on `/map/` too**, behind a `filled gaps` sidebar toggle (`roamly_show_inferred`). They are drawn to read as *part of the track*: the same per-device colour and dot size as a recorded fix, plus a detached ring in a colour used nowhere else (`#4fc3d9` cyan — not in the device palette, and distinct from the mauve snapped-point ring), with a dashed connecting line that respects `roamly_connect_lines`. `addInferredLayers` is idempotent and wired to both `style.load` and `styledata`, and the layers join `baseLayerIds()` so replay hides them. `/api/inferred/` returns `device_id` per batch for exactly this colouring.

`tracker/editor_tasks.py` holds the operations. Output goes to **`InferredLocation`** by default — no aggregate reads that table, so distance, visits, stats and fog are untouched by anything drawn. Generated points **are backed up** (`meta.version` **21**, the `edit_batches` section from `backup_tasks._build_edit_batches_data`): one entry per still-in-effect `target='inferred'` batch with its points, restored as a fresh `EditBatch` so each edit stays undoable as a unit, idempotent on the batch's first point. They are small and tedious to redraw, which outweighed calling them regenerable. **"Count as real data"** (offered when pasting or adding) instead writes plain `Location` rows, which are already covered by the backup format, so **`meta.version` is unaffected**. Every action records an **`EditBatch`** so it can be undone as a unit; for a paste-as-real the created ids are stored in `EditBatch.location_ids`, which is how the undo works without adding a column to `Location`.

**The copy guard matters:** `Location.unique_together` is `(device, latitude, longitude, timestamp)`, so pasting onto a *different date* collides with nothing and inserts cleanly — silently producing two interleaved tracks that every aggregate double-counts. `editor_copy_preview_api` therefore reports conflicts up front and `editor_copy_api` refuses a non-empty destination unless `replace` is set (which trashes the originals first).

**The `bulk_create` trap:** `bulk_create`/`update`/`bulk_update` all bypass `Location.save()`, leaving the PostGIS `location` column NULL and the point invisible to every spatial query — already a production bug once. Every editor path that creates or moves a real point goes through `editor_tasks._make_location`, which sets `Point(lng, lat, srid=4326)` explicitly; `editor_move_api` uses `.save()` rather than `.update()` for the same reason.

**Trash** (`TrashedLocation`). Deleted **real** points are snapshotted in full before deletion and restorable from Diagnostics → **Trash**; generated points are hard-deleted, being regenerable. A full snapshot rather than a tombstone is necessary because a `Location` is the primary record and cannot be recomputed — unlike `DismissedSuggestion`, whose "restore" merely clears a marker. Restoring mints a **new** id (`original_id` is informational) and is idempotent, since the recreated row carries the original unique tuple. Purged after `log_cleanup_tasks.TRASH_RETENTION_DAYS` (30) by the **existing hourly log-cleanup sweep** — deliberately not a new daemon, having just removed one. `TrashedLocation` with `reason='deleted'` is **excluded from backups**, as are paste-as-real and `delete` batches (their points are ordinary `Location` rows, and their undo ids cannot survive a restore); generated points and their batches are included (above).

**Single missed-fix auto fill** (`tracker/gapfill_tasks.py`, migration `0116`). The phone sometimes misses exactly one fix, leaving a 20-45 s hole in a drive. An hourly daemon (advisory-lock namespace **`0x52414d49` 'RAMI'**, per-user, cursor `UserProfile.gapfill_scan_last_id`, started from `apps.ready()`) drops **one inferred point at the halfway point of the road route** between the two neighbours, timestamped at the midpoint. **Inferred only**: an `EditBatch` of kind `auto` into `InferredLocation`, so it shows under "filled gaps", is undoable from the editor and never reaches distance/visits/stats. **The road gate is the safety story and the pass does nothing without it:** both neighbours must come back from `roads.snap_points` (which already refuses walks/stills/planes/trains and anything with no road inside its tolerance), `route_between` must return a route, and that route must be <= 1.5x the straight line. Also required: 25 m <= hop <= 45 m/s x gap, no `suspect`/accuracy > 100 m fixes, and no existing non-undone inferred point inside the gap (re-scans are harmless). Skipped entirely for accounts with `snap_to_roads` off or no resolved provider (cursor left alone). First scan starts 30 days back; at most 20 chunks of 5000 rows per sweep. Backups carry the batches already (kind is in `KIND_CHOICES`), so no `meta.version` bump. Tests: `tracker/tests/test_gapfill.py`.
