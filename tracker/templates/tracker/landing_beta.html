Your phone already knows where you've been. Keep it for yourself.

Roamly is a GPS journal you run on your own server. It logs your days, draws them on a map, adds up the miles and the time you spent in each place, and none of it goes anywhere else.

Start your own log or read how it works

Why bother running it yourself

It doesn't phone home.Your points sit in your own Postgres, your own Docker volume and, if you want backups, your own S3 bucket. There's no telemetry and no analytics beacon. The only server Roamly talks to is the one you started.

Place names without asking anyone.In the US, points are looked up against bundled Census boundaries, so a town is the one that actually contains the point, not whichever city is nearest. There's no Google call per point and no rate limit.

Made to keep going.Distance ignores GPS jitter, stats are snapshotted overnight, the Android app survives Doze, and backups can be restored twice without making a mess. It isn't flashy. It just keeps logging.

What's in it

One Django app, a native Android client, and whatever you already use to push locations.

  • Map

    Speed-coloured tracks, heatmaps, a day-by-day replay, and a scratch map of every country and US state you've been to. Vector tiles on PostGIS, classic mode on SQLite.

  • Adventures

    Shared trips with a day log, a freeform story and rated places with photos. Everyone's own route shows in its own colour on one map. You can share it publicly, behind a PIN if you like.

  • Stats and visits

    Distance is resampled and gated against pocket drift and GPS teleports, and time spent in a place gets the same care. Results are cached overnight, so the page opens instantly.

  • Search and places

    Search by place name, date phrase or radius. Draw your own named geofences and get top cities, time spent and a sample track for each.

  • Journals

    One entry a day with mood, weather and up to 20 photos, with that day's track pulled from your own history. Streaks and lifetime totals included.

  • Diagnostics

    A quality pass flags bad fixes (impossible speeds, altitude spikes, poor accuracy) on a map you can clean up point by point, plus a coverage timeline for the whole history.

  • Ask

    Ask questions about your own history in plain language, using any OpenAI-compatible model you point it at. Read-only, your own key.

  • Android app

    A foreground service that streams while the phone is awake and uses exact alarms once Doze takes over. Sync is offline-first and catches up by itself.

  • Your server

    Docker Compose, SQLite or PostgreSQL with PostGIS, S3-compatible backups, and imports from CSV, GPX, Takeout, OwnTracks and KML.

Up and logging in about two minutes

Compose starts the database, runs migrations and brings up the web app.

  1. Grab it and start it

    This pulls the prebuilt waxn/roamly image.

    curl -O https://raw.githubusercontent.com/waxn/roamly/main/docker-compose.yml
    docker compose up -d

  2. Make an account and an API key

    Open your instance, sign up, then go to Settings → API Keys to make one for your phone.

  3. Send it some locations

    Point GPSLogger's custom URL or OwnTracks at /api/push/ with your key. Garmin and Strava exports work through CSV import.

    curl -X POST localhost:8001/api/push/ \ -H "Authorization: Bearer YOUR_KEY" \ -d '{"device_id":"phone","latitude":45.5,"longitude":-122.6}'

  4. Go and look

    Browse your map, let the geocoder run, check how long you really spend places, start an adventure and invite family.

  5. Keeping it up to date

    Pull and restart. No clone, no rebuild.

    docker compose pull && docker compose up -d
