Your phone already knows where you've been. Keep it yours.

Roamly is a GPS journal you run on your own server. It logs your days, draws them on a map, and adds up the miles and the time you spent in each place. None of it goes anywhere else.

A day in the log

This is roughly what Roamly writes down for you while you get on with things.

07:12

Left the house. Phone in the left pocket, as always.Every fix is stored on your server the moment it arrives, or as soon as you're back online.

07:31

Coffee. You said ten minutes. It was forty.Roamly works out how long you stayed from where the dots stop moving, so there's nothing to check in to.

09:05

The library, by the long way round.Distance is counted with GPS jitter and teleporting fixes thrown out, so standing still doesn't earn you a kilometre.

13:40

Lunch on a bench by the river. Fifty-two minutes of nothing, perfectly recorded.Places you keep going back to can be named and turned into geofences with their own stats.

17:15

The lake, as the light goes.All of it stayed on your server, none of it went to a map company, and you can ask it questions about the day tomorrow.

It keeps your places to itself.

It never phones home. Your points sit in your own Postgres, your own Docker volume and, if you want backups, your own S3 bucket. No telemetry, no analytics beacon. The only server Roamly talks to is the one you started.

Place names without asking anyone. In the US, points are looked up against bundled Census boundaries, so a town is the one that actually contains the point, not whichever city is nearest. No per-point call to Google, and no rate limit.

Made to keep going. Gated distance, overnight stat snapshots, Android capture that survives Doze, backups you can restore twice without making a mess. It isn't flashy. It just keeps logging.

What's in it

Map. Speed-coloured tracks, heatmaps, a day-by-day replay, and a scratch map of every country and US state you've been to. Vector tiles on PostGIS, classic mode on SQLite.

Adventures. Shared trips with a day log, a freeform story and rated places with photos. Everyone's own route shows in its own colour on one map. Share it publicly, behind a PIN if you like.

Stats and visits. Distance is resampled and gated against pocket drift and GPS teleports, and time spent in a place gets the same care. Cached overnight, so the page opens instantly.

Search and places. Search by place name, date phrase or radius. Draw your own named geofences and get top cities, time spent and a sample track for each.

Journals. One entry a day with mood, weather and up to 20 photos, with that day's track pulled from your own history. Streaks and lifetime totals included.

Diagnostics. A quality pass flags bad fixes (impossible speeds, altitude spikes, poor accuracy) on a map you can clean up point by point, plus a coverage timeline for the whole history.

Ask. Put questions to your own history in plain language, using any OpenAI-compatible model you point it at. Read-only, with your own key.

Android app. A foreground service that streams while the phone is awake and uses exact alarms once Doze takes over. Sync is offline-first and catches up by itself.

Your server. Docker Compose, SQLite or PostgreSQL with PostGIS, S3-compatible backups, and imports from CSV, GPX, Takeout, OwnTracks and KML.

Up and logging in about two minutes

Compose starts the database, runs migrations and brings up the web app.

  1. Grab it and start it

    This pulls the prebuilt waxn/roamly image.

    curl -O https://raw.githubusercontent.com/waxn/roamly/main/docker-compose.yml
    docker compose up -d

  2. Make an account and an API key

    Open your instance, sign up, then go to Settings → API Keys to make one for your phone.

  3. Send it some locations

    Point GPSLogger's custom URL or OwnTracks at /api/push/ with your key. Garmin and Strava exports work through CSV import.

    curl -X POST localhost:8001/api/push/ \ -H "Authorization: Bearer YOUR_KEY" \ -d '{"device_id":"phone","latitude":45.5,"longitude":-122.6}'

  4. Go and look

    Browse your map, let the geocoder run, check how long you really spend places, start an adventure and invite family.

  5. Keeping it up to date

    Pull and restart. No clone, no rebuild. The full setup guide →

    docker compose pull && docker compose up -d

Go on, log something.

Free, yours, and it'll still be running next year.
